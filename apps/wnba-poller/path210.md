# Notes For Model

Purpose: This file is a decision log used to compare a decision currently under consideration against past outcomes, so we can learn which factors drive right vs. wrong binary decisions. Every decision resolves later to "right" or "wrong".

Guiding principle: the goal is NOT to always be right — that is impossible, outcomes carry irreducible variance and even a well-made decision can lose. The goal is to make decisions with a HIGH LIKELIHOOD OF NOT BEING WRONG by avoiding the patterns of behavior that have led to wrong outcomes in the past. Judge a decision (and a lean) by whether it steers away from the recurring losing shapes in this log — not by whether any single result landed. A sound decision that loses is still sound; a lucky win off a known losing pattern is still a bad process. Optimize process against the accumulated patterns, not individual outcomes.

Note on "trends": the recurring edges in this log are HIGH win-rate SPOTS, not certainties — a high-% spot carries a GUARANTEED share of losses, so we do NOT expect any trend to "hold" every time. A pattern "delivering" is the expected case; a pattern "breaking" on a single game is usually the built-in loss rate showing up, NOT proof the edge is gone. It is fine to describe a result as "a trend held/broke," but do not treat holding as owed or breaking as disproof: weight the accumulated sample over the latest result — don't abandon a sound high-% spot off one loss, and don't over-trust one just because it has hit recently.

1. The 'Past Events' section contains content split by 2 line breaks. Each chunk follows this schema:
name: string id starting with a number
result: either "right" or "wrong"
tags: single comma-separated line of strings (human-provided and model-generated tags are merged here, deduplicated into one normalized set)
line movement: optional single line, present only when timed numeric line values were provided. It is a comma-separated list of the line/price values in chronological order, each annotated with how long before the game it was observed (e.g. "-125 (2d), -128 (1d)"). If more than one market was tracked (e.g. a side and a total), separate them with a semicolon and label each (e.g. "side: ...; total: ..."). Times use d=days, h=hours, m=minutes before the game, plus "close" for the closing line.
context: string starting with "context" which is explanation of why the decision was made. It should capture the setup/reasoning for the decision, what moved (line/price), the action taken, the result, and the retrospective lesson (the "should have..."). When an event is promoted from '# Upcoming Events' to '# Past Events', the context must BEGIN with the day of the week the event occurred, lowercase and followed by a period (e.g. "context: friday. fade belgium = ..."), before the rest of the explanation, so day-of-week patterns can be tracked.
model_lean: optional single line, present when the decision came from '# Upcoming Events' and the model had recorded a lean. Briefly summarize the model's pre-game lean per market — side, total, parlay, or whatever markets were in the pick — each with its direction, strength (strong/moderate/small/pass), and whether it HIT or MISSED once resolved. This captures model-lean accuracy in a compact, structured form; the prose context lesson can still elaborate. STAR RATING (per-leg conviction, independent of strength): every generated lean supplies a `stars` object giving each bettable leg a 1–3 CONVICTION rating — 1 = lean, 2 = a real read, 3 = top conviction — that is DELIBERATELY SEPARATE from `strength` (which is stake/aggressiveness). A leg can be `small` strength yet 2-star conviction (e.g. a convicted fade of a firm line sized down for variance). Stars are captured at generation (persisted inside the revision summary as a `[stars: side=2, total=1, fh_total=2]` token) and the resolver deterministically stamps a `stars: <leg>=<n>:<result>` fragment onto model_lean at resolution (e.g. "... | stars: side=2:wrong, total=1:right"); pre-star leans fall back to strength. Never back-edit a star after the result. `wnba-poller star-record` tallies right/wrong per star tier straight from those fragments.

2. tags: read each entry's content and generate/merge tags that make sense given all the other contexts. There is ONE tags line per entry — fold any human-supplied tags into the same normalized vocabulary rather than keeping a separate list. Prefer a consistent, reusable vocabulary so the same factor uses the same tag across entries (this is what makes patterns accumulate). Rules for generating/regenerating tags:
- Regenerate across the whole set, not just the new entry. When a new decision is logged, re-derive tags for ALL entries together so the vocabulary stays normalized.
- Rebuild the '# Model Cache' after any (re)generation, since its counts are derived from tags and must stay in sync.
- Only ever edit the tags line. Never modify name, result, or context — those are the source of truth.
- Tag format/quality: lowercase snake_case; each tag must be a reusable factor that could recur in a future decision (not a one-off description); never encode the outcome as a tag (no "wrong"/"loss"); consolidate near-duplicates into one canonical tag.
- Derive tags from context, independent of result, so tags describe the decision's factors rather than hindsight.

- Flag recurring factors associated with wrong decisions.
- Give a lean, including the reasoning behind it and whether it is a strong or weak lean.
- Record the lean, its reasoning, and its strength over time so the model's own decision-making can be evaluated too.

KEEP and apply them as cautionary heuristics — the qualitative pattern can still hold for WNBA — but note that any right/wrong COUNT cited inline in these bullets (e.g. "chased_better_payout is 0/6", "fade_line_movement is 2/7") is SOCCER-ERA / mixed and is NOT the current WNBA-only cache figure. For an actual WNBA decision, pull the factor record from the '# Model Cache' above; treat these bullets as reusable cross-sport cautions, not sport-specific stats.
- (sometimes right) Nervousness is not inherently a negative signal. The winning entries (5, 6) were both nervous_underdog_backing, so expect that the right amount of nervousness can correlate with good outcomes. When weighing a lean, treat the degree of nervousness as its own factor rather than a reason to avoid a bet — mild/healthy nervousness about a sound read can be a positive pattern, while its absence (overconfidence) has been a loss signal.
- (usually right) Treat late line movement with skepticism for noise. Moves inside the last few hours before a game are often volatility, not signal. Weight the CLOSING line and the NET move (open -> close), not a transient intraday spike. A big-looking swing that reverts by close is effectively a stable line and should be tagged line_stable, NOT follow_/fade_line_movement — see entry 15, where mexico ran +104 open -> -104 (1h) but CLOSED +101, so the "-104 favorite" move was noise that should have been ignored. Do not overweight a move that closes near where it opened, and be most cautious about moves that only appear in the final hours.
- (usually right) Line direction is the strongest signal in the log. Backing the side or total the market is moving TOWARD (follow_line_movement, 3/1) is the most reliable winning template — see entries 5, 6, 8, 19. The mirror is the losing shape: backing an underdog while the line moves toward the favorite has repeatedly failed (e.g. entry 18). fade_line_movement is 2/7 and has only won when a strong fundamental or health/class mismatch justified betting against the move (entries 14, 20) — do not fade movement on narrative alone.
- (sometimes right; needs more data — update after more favorite flips) A favorite flip is meaningful, but do not scale confidence mechanically with the size of the move. Once a line crosses zero and extends 3-4 points, price overshoot becomes possible: prefer the new favorite's moneyline over chasing the spread, retain independent class analysis, and cap confidence when movement occurred during a data gap. Entry 92: the aces moved from -1 to +3 as the dream became the favorite, yet las vegas won by 22. Large movement is evidence, not proof. Revisit and update this lesson whenever another favorite flip resolves.
- (to consider; work in progress; needs more data — update as more market-vs-outcome examples resolve) Treat the line as a MARKET-CLEARING PRICE, not a pure bookmaker prediction of the most likely final margin. One practical sportsbook objective is to attract action at prices that manage/balance exposure; the posted spread therefore reflects bettor decision-making, demand, risk, and information — not only "what the book thinks the final score will be." Slightly consider the meta question: "What number will drive the betting behavior the market/book wants?" Entry 93 is a candidate example: storm +7 may have been the price that attracted balanced action even though liberty's likely class-based margin was closer to 11-12 (liberty won by 12). Entry 92 is another: the market moved from aces -1 to dream -3, a price that strongly redirected betting toward atlanta, yet las vegas won by 22. Practical use: compare the market-clearing number with an independent matchup/class projection; when they diverge sharply, do not assume the line is the literal expected margin or let movement erase the fundamental read. This is a secondary lens, NOT a reason to automatically fade the book — line direction still carries information, and these examples need a larger sample.
- (almost always right) Treat chasing a bigger payout as a HIGH-ALERT warning. chased_better_payout is 0/6 — the worst record in the log. Taking a larger-return expression (a run line / big spread / parlay) over the simple moneyline or side has not cashed once (entries 3, 7, 9, 13, 17). Default to the simplest line expression; when a bigger-payout number is tempting, treat it as a strong signal to step back, since the extra payout reflects the added margin the market is pricing against you. NOTE the mirror-image trap, which is DISTINCT from chasing payout: OVER-INSURING a confirmed side by paying up for a safer cushion — e.g. laying heavy juice for a -1.5/+1.5 spread (paying -158 or -195) instead of taking the moneyline. Chasing payout reaches for a BIGGER return (a plus-money longshot expression); over-insuring accepts a WORSE return (heavy minus juice) to reduce variance. Both give back the edge and both are wrong defaults, but do not tag or describe over-insurance as chased_better_payout — it is the opposite direction. When the read is that a side simply WINS, the moneyline is the correct expression: don't reach past it for a bigger-payout spread (chased_better_payout), and don't pay up past it for a safety cushion (over-insurance) either.
- (usually right) Tipster signals have inverted in the log. followed_tipster is 0/4 (entries 1, 6, 13, 15) while faded_tipster is 2/1 (wins in entries 8, 14). Tailing a single tipster — especially one fresh off a win — has not cashed; fading one has a positive record. Treat "a tipster is on this" as a mild reason to fade rather than follow, not as a standalone signal. NOTE the difference between "a tipster" and "lots of tipsters": one tipster's pick is the faded_tipster/followed_tipster signal above, whereas broad agreement (many tipsters / the market consensus) is a SEPARATE factor tracked by follow_consensus (1/2) and fade_consensus (1/3), which are mixed and weaker — do not treat a single sharp's opinion and a crowd consensus as the same thing.
- (sometimes right) Situational/narrative angles need line confirmation. situational_angle is 3/7 — it has only won when the angle ALIGNED with line movement or reflected a real class/health mismatch (entries 8, 19, 20). A pure narrative ("revenge spot", "should destroy", "shootout") with no line support has consistently lost (entries 9, 10, 13, 15, 16). CRUCIALLY, a narrative angle that points AGAINST the line movement is typically a wrong angle — the market moving the other way is evidence the narrative is mistaken (e.g. entry 18, where the "revenge spot" pointed one way but the line moved toward the other side and the angle lost). Require an angle to be backed by line direction or a concrete edge before weighting it; if the line disagrees with your narrative, trust the line.
- (sometimes right) A big underdog COMING OFF A BLOWOUT LOSS can RISE TO THE OCCASION and keep it close, especially against a favorite riding high off a win. A team humiliated last time out (e.g. a 40+ point loss) tends to play with pride/urgency as a big dog, while a favorite off a big win can come out flat/overconfident (a let-down), so the dog covers the big number (or better). fademercury 42: phoenix, a +11.5 dog the day after a 48-point blowout loss (entry 36), facing a lynx team off a win and "destroying everyone," LOST BY ONLY 4 (104-100), covering +11.5 easily. practical read: be wary of LAYING a big number against a just-blown-out dog facing a hot favorite — lean toward the +points dog (or at least do NOT back the favorite's cover); this is a specific flavor of the big-favorite-doesn't-cover pattern (fadestorm 25, fadesky 39, fadesparks 41, fademercury 42).
- (usually right) Distrust strong conviction formed days before with no line movement. overconfidence is 0/3 and decision_day_before is 1/4. High certainty locked in days out — especially "definitely the better team" / "bet of the tournament" reads — has underperformed, and when the line stays flat (line_stable, 0/2) there is no market confirmation of the edge (entry 10's lesson; also entries 12, 13, 15). A firm early opinion that the market never validates is a fade sign, not a green light.
- (sometimes right) Point-total OVERs in the WNBA are a SOFT caution, not a ban — we CAN take the over on occasion when there are good signs or a situational pattern. total_over is now 8/8 (up from 5/6): the caution has weakened as overs have hit in a recent run, so do NOT reflexively fade the over. The genuine risk is endgame variance in a likely BLOWOUT on a LOW total: WNBA garbage time (intentional fouling, deliberate clock-milking, fewer possessions, bench lineups) can strand an over so the last few minutes produce almost no points (entry 25 fadestorm landed 167 under u170; entry 26 fadeaces 168 under o175.5). DEFAULT on a nondescript total: lean UNDER or PASS, and when unsure PASS rather than force an under. TAKE THE OVER when a concrete sign or pattern supports it: (a) a RISING total / money onto the over — do NOT mechanically fade a total the market is pushing UP; that was exactly the recent, repeated mistake (entries 38 fadeliberty 184, 39 fadesky 187, 40 fadeaces 184 all went over after the model faded/passed a high-or-rising total), and a rising total is a follow_line_movement signal, the log's best. (b) a real "no-defense / high-pace" matchup where both teams defend poorly (entries 29 fadewings 203, 31 fadesky 189, 38 fadeliberty 184 all cashed the over). (c) a competitive-game expectation, since the stranding risk is worst in likely blowouts on low totals — a high total between fast teams can still clear even in a blowout (entry 40 fadeaces 184 over o182.5). Side/total tiebreak still applies but is softer: when a side and total lean both exist and you can pick only one, default to the side, BUT a well-signaled over (rising total + no-defense matchup) is now a legitimate standalone lean, not an automatic drop. ALL-STAR-BREAK WINDOW (EXPIRED — do NOT apply): there was a time-bounded over-tilt right around the WNBA all-star break (fadedream 35, fadeliberty 38, fadesky 39, fadeaces 40, fadesparks 41 all ran high-scoring / over), but that break ended weeks ago and this window CLOSED as of the 6-game Wednesday slate (entries 67-72, ~17 days before 2026-08-08) — it should NOT have been cited on any lean since, and several recent leans wrongly leaned or tempered toward the over on it. Do NOT tilt toward the over for the all-star break, and do NOT treat "don't lean under in blowout spots" as active — that override is retired. Fresh evidence confirms the reversion to normal scoring: across the 2026-08-05/06 slates 6 of 7 games landed UNDER the closing total and over-leans went 0/3. Revert to the DEFAULT above — lean UNDER or PASS on a nondescript total, take the over only on a concrete sign (rising total / no-defense matchup / competitive high total), never on a seasonal/break tilt. Those games are KEPT in the Model Cache counts, but when weighing a TOTAL lean read the total_over / total_under record BOTH including and excluding them (the split is in the Model Cache); the excluding-window numbers are the cleaner prior now that the tilt is retired.
- (sometimes right) Cross-book total disagreement can flag a sharp side. In entry 31 (fadesky) BetOnline's total closed ~2 points HIGHER than HardRock's. When BetOnline sits a full ~2 points off other books at CLOSING, treat that as a possible SHARP BIAS toward that side of the total (here, the higher number pointing at the over — which cashed, 189). Practical read: a ~2-pt closing gap between BetOnline and the field is a signal to lean toward the side BetOnline is on, or at minimum not to bet against it; watch for this cross-book divergence at close, not just a single book's own drift.
- (to consider; NEW — work in progress, accumulate examples before trusting) FIRST-HALF vs FULL-GAME divergence carries a total (and a weaker spread) signal — check it on every game where BetOnline posts a first-half market, and fold it into the lean. Baseline: a first-half total normally sits near HALF the full-game total, and a first-half spread near HALF the full-game spread. When they diverge NOTICEABLY from that baseline, note the direction and size:
  - TOTAL (the stronger signal): compare the 1H total to full_total / 2. A 1H total set SEVERAL POINTS BELOW half the full total means the market is pricing a lower-scoring, slower/half-court first half than a straight split — read it as support for the UNDER (a first-half under, and more weakly the full-game under, on the logic that a low-pace/defensive start tends to cap the game). The mirror — 1H total ABOVE half the full total — tilts toward the OVER / a fast start. Size it by the gap: tag `fh_total_low_big` / `fh_total_high_big` when the 1H total is roughly 3+ points off half the full total, `fh_total_low_medium` / `fh_total_high_medium` for roughly 1.5–3 points off; under ~1.5 is noise, no tag. CAVEAT: a low 1H total can instead mean a BACK-LOADED game (slow start, scoring later) rather than a true under, so treat this as a supporting cross-check against full-game total movement, not an override.
  - SPREAD (usually smaller divergence): compare the 1H spread to full_spread / 2. It normally tracks half closely, so any real gap is worth noting: a 1H spread WIDER than half the full spread says the market expects the favorite to build its lead EARLY (front-loaded — the favorite may coast/fade late, so it supports the first-half favorite and can weaken a full-game favorite cover), tag `fh_spread_front`; a 1H spread NARROWER than half suggests a SLOW favorite start / second-half team, tag `fh_spread_back`. Only tag when the gap is beyond rounding (~1.5+ pts).
  - Only act on divergence that is REAL — a book actually posted the first-half market and the gap clears the thresholds; when no first-half market exists there is no signal.
  - ALWAYS SURFACE THE CHECK (visibility requirement): every lean must state the first-half read EXPLICITLY and up front in the summary — even when there is NO divergence — so it is always clear the first half was considered, not just when it produced a lean. Use a short "1H check:" line giving the numbers and the verdict, e.g. "1H check: total 86.5 vs half 89.5 = -3.0 -> fh_total_low_big (under); spread 1H -1 vs half -1.25 = noise". When the halves line up, say so plainly (e.g. "1H check: total and spread both track half -> no edge"); when BetOnline has not posted a first-half market, say "1H check: no first-half market posted". Never omit the 1H check from a lean.
- (usually right) A total that barely moves is a NO-SIGNAL total — do not read its tiny drift as an "under" cue. In entry 29 (fadewings) the total moved only ~2 points net across the whole window (o180.5 -> o178.5), was essentially flat in the first 24 hours (o180.5 -> o180) and dead flat in the last 6 hours (o178.5 held from 6h to tip); the only movement was a single small tick between 1d and 6h. That is a stable/inactive total (line_stable behavior), NOT a real "money onto the under" move, yet it got read as a mild under lean — and the game blew OVER (203 vs o178.5). Practical rule: when a total's net move is small (~2 pts) AND it is flat in both the opening 24h and the closing 6h, treat it as line_stable with no directional signal and do NOT bet the under off it; a nearly-flat total gives no cover to fade the over.
- (usually right) Take available hedges and avoid self-conflicting tickets. missed_hedge is 0/2 (entries 2, 7 — passing on an available ~50% hedge hurt) and parlay_conflict is 0/2 (entries 4, 13 — legs that fought each other or an existing position). When a reasonable mid-game or pre-game hedge is available, lean toward taking it, and avoid stacking a bet that conflicts with another position you already hold. Some games present a HIGH likelihood of an in-game hedge opportunity in the first half (which is ideal — an early swing lets you lock value): watch for high-emotion spots (a "game of the tournament", rivalry/revenge games, elimination matches) where volatile early scoring/live-line swings make a first-half hedge especially likely, and plan for it going in rather than reacting late.
- (sometimes right) A reliable fade signal that ALSO has a Trent bet on it is a strong fade candidate. When a factor with a poor track record points against a bet AND Trent (a single tipster) is on that same bet, the two negatives compound. In entry 24 (fadeegypt) the -1.5 was a chased_better_payout (0/7, the worst tag) AND trent was on the -1.5 (followed_tipster 0/5) — both signals said fade, and the -1.5 lost. Treat "a known losing signal + a Trent pick on the same side" as a high-confidence spot to fade that side (or at minimum avoid backing it), since faded_tipster (2/1) beats followed_tipster (0/5) and stacking it on top of an already-weak factor makes the fade cleaner.
